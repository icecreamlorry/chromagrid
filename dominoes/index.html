How to play

The idea

The double-six set is 28 tiles. You each get 7; the remaining 14 form the boneyard. Be first to play your last tile — or, if the game blocks, hold the fewest pips.

The opening

Whoever holds the highest double leads with it. If neither of you has a double, the heaviest tile leads. That tile is the only one playable on the opening move, so it is chosen for you.

Playing a tile

A tile may only go on an end whose pip count it matches. Playable tiles in your rack are marked with a ▲ and a dashed outline. If a tile fits both ends you'll be asked which end you want.

Drawing & passing

If you cannot play, you must draw from the boneyard — one tile at a time — until you can. Only when the boneyard is empty and you still cannot play may you pass. The buttons appear only when the rules allow them.

Ending & scoring

Play your last tile and you win, scoring the pips left in your opponent's hand. If both players pass in a row with an empty boneyard the game is blocked: the lighter hand wins and scores the heavier one. Level pips is a draw.

Per-move timer

The host picks how long each side gets per move — from 1 minute to 3 days, or Unlimited. Run out on your move and you lose on time.

Confirm moves

By default moves are staged — pick a tile and end to preview it, then press Confirm. Turn it off in the menu to play immediately.
